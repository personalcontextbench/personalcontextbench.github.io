What these numbers mean

Each system was asked 113 everyday requests. A fact counts as remembered when it ended up in the answer. Rounded to whole facts out of 10, the exact shares are: ChatGPT memory* 91%, IntentOne 89%, Claude memory* 81%, Hermes Agent 66%, search over messages 59%, and MIRIX on screenshots 24%

OpenClaw, an open-source agent like Hermes Agent, used 8%. Set up as the person's own assistant, as its docs suggest, it used 15% of the facts in their AI chats, where ChatGPT memory* used 97% and IntentOne 91%. Its search marks each saved day down by its age, so a fact said more than about 45 days before a request rarely comes back, and most facts here are older than that

The search systems and both agents lose most facts at step 3: the fact is stored, but it isn't found when it's needed

Handing the AI the whole history with every request, which is not a memory, reached 88%, at 40 times the price

Each number is out of the facts that memory could see: 145 for memories that read AI chats and messages, 156 for screen memories. Out of all 156, ChatGPT memory* used 85% and IntentOne 83%

Tested request by request, ChatGPT memory*, IntentOne, Claude memory* and putting the whole history in the prompt aren't apart from each other, and each beats every search and screen memory. Hermes Agent is behind ChatGPT memory*, IntentOne and the whole history, and not apart from search over messages. Every other system beats OpenClaw

With two people this holds for these two, not yet as a ranking

What we know so far

We made one of our two people's 5 months busier, with more contacts and more messages: from about 1,700 messages to 3,400, 6,700 and 16,600, or 2, 4 and 10 times as many. At 16,600 messages the history is about 1.4 million tokens

ChatGPT memory* went from 90% to 84% at 4 times, within chance, then to 62% at 10 times, a real drop. It still had 96% of the facts stored, but found the right ones only 63% of the time. It can hand only about 6,000 tokens of notes to each answer, and at 10 times its notes likely outgrow that room

Claude memory* went from 81% to 56% to 20% at 4 times. It writes facts down as well as ever, but its notes have to stay under 1,000 words, so each rewrite likely has to drop more. We didn't run it at 10 times

IntentOne, search over messages and both screen memories stayed within chance of where they started at every size. Handing over the whole history held to 4 times, but at 10 times it no longer fits in what the AI can read at once

All of this is one made-up person so far. The memory growth section below has every system

Why screens did worse

The same ChatGPT memory*, given screenshots of the person's screen instead of their messages, used 89 facts instead of 132, and cost twice as much: $1.98 versus $1.02 a month per person

From screens, the memory wrote down 149 of the 156 facts, more than from messages. But its notes grew more than 20 times longer

It can hand the AI only about 4,500 words at a time (6,000 tokens). Past that, it hands over only the lines that share words with the request. So of the 62 facts it learned but missed, 56 never reached the AI

Reading the exact screen text did no better (87) than reading screenshots with OCR (89). Search points the same way: 86 facts from messages, 80 from screens

Our screens are cleaner than real desktops, so this is a best case for screens

Where the people and messages come from

Each synthetic person is generated from a hidden list of true facts: 8 contacts with 3 or 4 facts each, plus 8 facts about the person, 36 in all, some of them changing over time

From that list come about 150 days of AI chats, chat and email with the contacts, and the pages and apps the person looked at. Of 1,679 messages each, most are ordinary filler, as in real life

We decide where each fact shows up, so how many facts each source holds is set by the design: AI chats alone show 91 of the 156 facts the requests need, adding messages to people shows 145, and screens show all 156

What we measure is how well a memory carries them. Of the facts each could see, ChatGPT memory* used 97% from AI chats alone and 91% with messages added, and IntentOne 91% and 89%

No real person's data is used

How each step is checked
Learn
Is the fact in any weekly snapshot of the memory
Store
Is it in the final memory with its current value. An old value counts as lost
Recall
Is it in what the memory handed the AI for this request
Use
Does the answer use it

Each check looks for the fact's key words. A fact said in other words counts as lost, and one named wrongly, negated or out of date can count as used. Two outside raters will check this

What the requests are

113 requests across the two people, needing 156 facts in all: questions about the past, facts that changed, drafting a message, everyday requests typed into an AI app, questions that need two sources, privacy checks, and questions with no answer

Every memory runs on Claude Haiku 5.5 and every answer on Claude Sonnet 5.5, so the differences come from the memory

How the bigger sizes are made

The same generator writes 5 months for a synthetic person built the same way, with more contacts and more messages: 16 contacts at twice the size and 32 at 4 times. At 10 times the messages come to about 1.4 million tokens

Every memory runs on the bigger history and is asked the same kinds of questions about it

How sure this is

The normal and 10 times sizes asked 54 questions of one person, the 2 and 4 times sizes 38. With so few, small changes can be chance. Search over messages' 6-point dip at 10 times could really be anything from 20 points down to 8 points up

The drop for ChatGPT memory* at 10 times is 27 points, and at least 15 even at the low end. The drop for Claude memory* at 4 times is 60 points, and at least 46. Neither is chance

At 10 times, IntentOne used 83% and ChatGPT memory* 62%. For this one made-up person that gap is bigger than chance: 16 facts only IntentOne used, 1 only ChatGPT memory*. IntentOne is also clearly ahead of search and both screen memories there. With one person, this is not yet a ranking

The bigger sizes happened to ask a different mix of questions, so each size is scored as if it had the normal size's mix. Before that correction, search over messages seemed to rise from 58% to 80% at 4 times. It doesn't

How we count the price

One person's month. The memory takes in 30 days of messages, about 340, and the AI answers 100 requests. We use list prices with no bulk discount, and leave out the test's own scoring

Two models share the work. Claude Haiku 5.5 does each memory's own AI work, like writing notes. Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes every answer, and for the same amount of text it costs 20 times as much. So most of the price is the answers, and a memory that hands the AI more text with each request costs more

  • ChatGPT memory*: about 1 cent of note-taking, plus $1.01 for 100 answers that each read about 4,300 tokens, $1.02 in all
  • Screen memory, exact text: no notes to write, but each answer reads about 10,500 tokens of screen text, $2.24
  • Whole history: each answer reads about 200,000 tokens, $41

Nothing was tuned to cost less. Every memory runs as designed, on the same two models, so these prices compare designs, not what an app would charge

Answers are priced without the discount for repeated text, since a person's requests come hours apart
